How it works

Three steps. No black box.

Every number on ModelXI traces back to real match data. Here's the whole pipeline, start to finish.

01

The model learns

A Dixon-Coles statistical model rates every team's attack and defence from thousands of real results across the last two seasons.

Recent form is weighted more heavily than old results, so a team's rating reflects who they are now — not who they were a year ago.
02

It prices every market

From that goal model it computes calibrated probabilities for the result, handicaps, totals, both-teams-to-score, correct scores and more.

One model, a dozen markets — all internally consistent, because they're derived from the same distribution of likely scores.
03

AI explains it

A language model turns the numbers into a short, readable preview — grounded strictly in the model's output and real form.

It's not allowed to invent a single fact. If the model didn't say it, the AI can't write it. Coin-flips are called honestly.

How accurate is it?

We don't ask you to trust a number.

We publish every result instead. This is the honest answer to "how good is it?"

Calibration, not bravado.

A prediction is only worth anything if its confidence is real. The test isn't whether we sound confident — it's whether a "70%" call actually happens about 70% of the time, over many matches.

"When the model says 65%, does it land 65% of the time? That's the only question that matters — and it's one you can check."

That's why every single call the model makes is graded against the real final score and shown publicly. No cherry-picking, no hidden misses. You judge us on the record.

FAQ

Straight answers.

Is this betting advice?

No. ModelXI is statistical analysis for information — probability estimates and reasoning. It doesn't tell you to place a bet and it doesn't guarantee outcomes. What you do with it is your call.

Do you guarantee wins?

No, and anyone who does is lying. Football is uncertain by nature. We estimate probabilities as honestly as we can and show our reasoning; we don't sell certainty.

How do I know it's actually accurate?

You watch the track record. Every call the model makes is graded against the real final score and shown publicly, including calibration — whether a 70% call really happens about 70% of the time. It builds as matches are played, so you can verify it yourself.

Which leagues are covered?

Eighteen at launch — the big European leagues plus Brazil, Argentina, MLS, Liga MX, Liga I and others. Each has enough history for the model to be credible. More as it proves out.

Why no corners, cards or injuries?

Because our model doesn't measure them — and we won't fake numbers we can't stand behind. Those need different data and different models. They're on the roadmap, honestly labelled as coming.

Is it free?

Yes, for now. If premium tiers arrive later, the core analysis stays honest either way.
